# Dino & Monster Word Arena

A separate, static, phone-friendly game in the OAI HTML gallery. Children choose a dinosaur or monster, switch among eleven challenges (Math, Math 2, colors, body parts, family, animals, fruit, vegetables, flowers, vehicles, and weather), and spar with a pretend face-off: each correct answer launches the champion's silly power move (Rex's Tail Swish or Bobo's Bubble Blast), the sparring buddy wobbles and giggles, and one of its power pips fades. When the buddy is out of power it takes a bow and high-fives the child. A wrong answer is a harmless pillow block, but it always costs something a child can see: it costs one **heart** (a match starts with 3 hearts on Easy, 4 on Harder, and 5 on Super, shown as ♥ ♥ ♥ at the top of the arena, where a small "♥ −1" floats up and the lost heart turns hollow), takes one star back from the current match (never below zero), and, if hearts remain, the child hears the right word and taps its pulsing item to continue. The match is **lost** when the last heart goes: any active word echo finishes (or the card appears immediately if there is no echo), then an "Out of hearts!" card offers **Try again**, which brings every heart back and starts the match over (no sticker, and no sticker already collected is ever taken away). Because only the first tap counts, tapping choices one after another cannot win a question. A wrong first tap still costs a heart and a star even though the child can tap the revealed correct item to continue; only correct first taps earn stars. No injuries, timed challenges, accounts, or saved answers. The game also has collectible stickers and unlockable costumes (see [Stickers and costumes](#stickers-and-costumes)).

The game is also installable as an offline-capable app (a web manifest and service worker at `monster-word-arena-tw/`): every picture and speech asset is cached automatically on first visit, so it stays fully playable offline afterward with no separate download step; GA4 requests are never cached and are skipped entirely while offline.

The page reveals itself a step at a time on every open: first only the champion picker, then (once a champion is tapped) the topic and level pickers plus a big **▶ Start!** button, and finally — once Start is tapped — the sparring arena and the first spoken question. That tap is also what turns sound on; no earlier tap plays audio, and the rule that sound starts only after this gesture is unchanged. At each of these steps, the chosen champion visibly bounces or points toward the next thing to tap (the champion cards, Start, the answers, Next power move, and the finish/break screens), with a short two-note chime; this is purely decorative (`aria-hidden`) and, like every animation in the game, is held still by `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. On a phone, tapping Start also brings the question and its answers smoothly into view and tucks the champion picker, intro tagline, and sticker/costume summary out of the way for the rest of that match, so nothing needs to be hunted for by scrolling.

After 2 or 3 match wins in a row (re-rolled each time, so it never feels mechanical), the finish screen offers a soft, equally-weighted choice instead of the usual single Play-again button: **One more round** or **Take a break**. Neither has a timer or a penalty — one more round simply starts the next match, and a break shows a calm goodbye screen with its own spoken line that can be left again at any time with one tap.

A small **⚙️ Grown-ups** icon in the header (separate from **↻ Start over**) opens a settings panel behind a simple check only an adult can solve quickly (two-digit addition, e.g. "17 + 27 = ?"; a wrong answer just offers a new one, and Enter submits the answer the same as tapping the on-screen Enter button, on desktop and on a phone or tablet's own keyboard). Once past it, a short line at the top explains what the panel is for, and every section below has its own one-line explanation of what it changes: which levels the level picker offers the child (at least one always stays on); the smaller second language shown under key text, or turning it off; whether the spoken narration uses a different language than the on-screen one; muting narration and sound effects; and how many matches have been won and stickers collected, with a button to clear the sticker book — the same reset that used to live inside the sticker book's own "For grown-ups" section, which is now just for browsing stickers and costumes. These choices are saved only on this device.

A level choice starts on **Easy**: the question is the written prompt (for example "Where is the dog?"), with no picture of the answer next to it, and there are three answer choices that are **pictures only** (a colour swatch for colors). **Harder** shows just the written word (in whichever on-screen text language is chosen) alongside the spoken word, with four picture-only choices and a five-star match. Because the word is on screen at those two levels, no choice repeats it as a label, so the child has to know which picture is which rather than match identical letters; each picture button keeps the word as its accessible name (`aria-label`) for screen readers. **Super** is listening-only: neither the picture nor the written word is shown, only a replay button and four choices from the full topic (picture and word, as before), in a ten-star match; if sound is off or unavailable, Super falls back to showing the written word so it stays playable. **Face/body-part questions are the exception at every level:** the child hears the body-part prompt and taps that part on one unlabelled character diagram, rather than choosing among separate icons. The character shows 18 body parts and each level asks about its own pool: **Easy** the original 8 (eyes, nose, ears, mouth, tooth, hair, hands, feet), **Harder** 13 (adding head, cheeks, arms, legs, tummy), and **Super** all 18 (adding neck, shoulders, knees, fingers, toes, which are small or sit under clothes). Only the parts a level asks about can be tapped, so a bigger level's part never takes a tap from the parts around it; a part that lies inside another counts for it (teeth for the mouth, fingers for the hands, toes for the feet, knees for the legs, shoulders for the arms, and any part of the face or hair for the head) but not the other way round. The named part is marked after an answer; if the diagram image cannot load, the question falls back to the usual picture choices (three on Easy, four on Harder and Super, always from that level's pool). Math is richer at every level, always with a number choice: **Easy** adds and takes away within five (answers 0 to 5; 10 sums and 14 take-aways), shown with the egg picture; a take-away shows every egg with the ones being taken away faded and crossed out, so the eggs left can still be counted, and it asks "How many are left?". **Harder** stays within ten and mixes counting eggs (5 to 10), addition (24 sums) and subtraction (27 take-aways, some ending at 0), with the egg picture. **Super** mixes the same plus and minus questions with no picture but always shows the equation. From Harder up, half of the number choices include the result of the wrong operation (for example 7 for "5 − 2") to catch a guess. The pools are large enough that the same few sums do not keep repeating (Easy 24 questions, Harder 57, Super 51), and plus and minus take turns: the same operation is never asked three times in a row, so about half of all math questions at every level are take-aways and any three questions in a row on Easy or Super include both a sum and a take-away. Switching the challenge during an active match keeps earned stars and lost hearts. Before a heart is lost, switching levels starts a fresh match at the selected level; after a heart is lost, changing levels abandons and loses that match. Whenever a match is lost this way, the lost-match card appears with no hearts remaining in the header before a fresh match can begin. Once that card is shown, choosing any level (including the active one) dismisses it and starts a fresh match there with full hearts; choosing a level after a win also starts a fresh match there. Start over during an undamaged match restarts it normally, while Start over after a miss shows the lost-match card first. Every level draws from the full word lists, except the body parts (see above): 8 colors, 8 to 18 body parts (by level), 7 family members, 8 animals, 8 fruits, 8 vegetables, 6 flowers, 6 vehicles, and 7 weather words. Questions are picked at random without asking the same question twice in a row.

Math shows countable eggs (counting questions and take-aways group them in fives) at Easy and Harder; word questions other than face/body-part and animal picture questions show no picture in the question, and their pictures appear only as the picture-only choices; those pictures are original illustrations. On first load, the child or a grown-up picks one language — English, 繁體中文, or にほんご (hiragana) — and that single choice sets both all on-screen text and the spoken narration; there is no separate first-load voice picker. Choosing a language is also the gesture that turns on sound, the same as tapping Start. Key labels, the question prompt, topic and level names, the reward bar, the sticker book, and the finish screen also show a smaller second language under the main text (for example "📒 Sticker book / 📒 貼紙本"), echoing the game's original bilingual pairing; English defaults to 繁體中文 as its second language, 繁體中文 defaults to English, and にほんご defaults to English. Answer choices stay single-language so they remain uncluttered. In the grown-up settings, a second language can be changed to either of the other two or turned off, and a separate, off-by-default setting lets the narration voice differ from the on-screen language. Japanese on-screen text (main or second) is written entirely in hiragana, including words normally spelled in katakana or kanji (for example とまと, でんしゃ), so a child who cannot yet read kanji or katakana can still read every word himself; this is separate from the spoken narration, which uses whatever spelling reads correctly aloud. English is the default language on a first visit; a language chosen on an earlier visit is remembered on this device (`localStorage` key `monsterWordArena.settings.v1`, holding the language, the second language and whether a grown-up set it directly, whether the narration voice is overridden and to what, whether sound is muted, and the grown-up level lock — no answers, questions, or names) and wins over the English default, though the same Start-button gesture (or the language picker itself) is still required each visit before any audio plays; an earlier visit's separate text and voice languages migrate sensibly, with the old text language becoming the new single language. The current question is spoken right away once sound turns on, and automatically when a new question appears, with replay and mute controls. Once narration is on, short spoken reactions in the voice language cheer a right answer and its power move, give a gentle try-again on a miss before echoing the right word and waiting for the second-chance tap, warn out loud when a miss leaves only one heart, say when the last heart is gone and it is time to try again, celebrate the final high-five, say after a capped win which way earns the next sticker (see [Stickers and costumes](#stickers-and-costumes)), invite the child to choose another round or a break, and say goodbye when the child takes a break; they follow the mute control and share the question's player, so the next question, a language change, level, topic, or champion switch, or a restart cuts off any reaction still playing. Speech is bundled as local MP3 assets, so the game makes no speech-service requests and remains playable if audio is unavailable.

The layout works from phone width up: a single column on phones and narrow tablets, two columns (arena beside the challenge) from tablet-landscape width up, and on common laptop screens (1366×768, 1440×900) the question and its answers fit on screen without scrolling once Start is tapped. On a phone in portrait, the arena stays pinned near the top of the screen while the question and answers scroll into easy reach beneath it.

The Pages deployment injects Google Analytics (GA4) and the shared interaction-analytics helper into every published HTML page (see the root README and `.github/workflows/pages.yml`); this page is **not tracking-free**, and general page-engagement analytics remain enabled. Answer choices are anonymous buttons with no analytics IDs, labels, or event declarations, so the helper does not report which answer a child chose. The sticker book, costume, and reset buttons are anonymous in the same way, so opening the book, earning a sticker, or changing a costume is not reported either.

The game saves one thing, only in this browser on this device: the `localStorage` key `monsterWordArena.rewards.v1`, holding the number of sticker-earning wins, each champion's chosen costume, and today's sticker tally per language and level, for example `{"v":2,"wins":3,"wearing":{"dino":"crown","monster":null},"daily":{"date":"2026-09-30","counts":{"ja:easy":2}}}`. The key keeps its `v1` name so earlier saves load in place: an earlier `{"v":1,"wins":3,"wearing":{...}}` value with no `daily` field is read as-is (no win, sticker, or unlocked costume is lost) and is rewritten in the newer shape the next time a win or a costume choice is saved. It never saves answer choices, questions, names, or anything that identifies the child, and it is never sent anywhere. At page load, a temporary `monsterWordArena.rewards.v1.probe` value is written and immediately removed to check that storage works. If the browser blocks storage, the game still plays and the stickers last only for the visit. A grown-up can erase the saved key from **⚙️ Grown-ups → Clear sticker book** (behind the settings check described above), which asks for a second tap to confirm; clearing the browser's site data also erases it.

### Hearing the word again

After every answer on a word question (colors, body, family, animals, fruit, vegetables, flowers, vehicles, weather; math has no word to say), the reaction is followed by the word said alone, in the narration voice, while the item that was named pulses (on a body picture question, the ring around the right part). A small 🔊 control under the feedback line says the word again. After a miss, tapping the pulsing correct item waits for the word to finish before moving on. After a correct answer, tapping Next before the echo starts waits until the word starts, then moves on and may cut the echo short; once the echo has started, Next can cut it short immediately. The very last answer of a match also echoes before the finish screen appears.

A wrong tap still costs its heart (and star), but it is now a second chance rather than a dead end: the child hears the right word, sees the right item pulse, and taps it to continue. Only the right item can be tapped; any other tap changes nothing. If nobody taps, the game still moves on by itself after a wait that starts over when the word has finished, so a longer clip never takes the turn away (about 9 seconds after the clips, or about 6 with sound off). When the last heart goes, any active echo finishes and the lost-match panel appears immediately if there is no echo.

A grown-up can turn on **Say the word aloud in a second language** in the settings (spoken, separate from the written second language above) (off by default, one language at a time, never the same as the narration voice). After a right answer only, the word is then also said in that second language, using the same word clips. The choice is saved as `echoLanguage` in the settings value; an older save without it keeps it off, and choosing that language as the narration voice turns it off again.

The word clips live in `audio/words.json` (76 words, id `word-<topic>-<word>`, in English, Taiwan Mandarin and Japanese) and are generated with the same script as the other clips. The Japanese text uses the spelling that reads correctly aloud (kanji for some original face words, kana for the ten added body parts, katakana for loanwords, お兄さん for brother), and the Mandarin `word-family-sister` and `word-animals-cat` inputs use 姐姐 and 小猫 for the same reasons as their prompts above. All 228 clips were transcribed back with whisper.cpp (large-v3-turbo, quantized) and, for Mandarin and Japanese, also by Gemini as pronounced; the 30 added body-part clips were checked by both methods in those languages. English matched for every word. Mandarin matched apart from traditional/simplified script and a few short one-syllable words the transcribers mishear (checked one by one with Gemini). In Japanese, four single-word clips were regenerated with a written form that transcribed back correctly: the `word-weather-snowy`, `word-weather-sunny` and `word-flowers-rose` inputs use 雪, 晴れ！ and 薔薇 (ゆき was heard as ゆうき, and はれ and ばら with a stretched vowel), and `word-face-tooth` uses 歯！, the closest to a plain は, which the transcribers still hear as a clipped はっ.

## Math 2

**Math 2** is a second number topic beside Math, with its own Easy / Harder / Super. Math is unchanged. Math 2 grows one kind of question at a time, drawing on the grade 1–2 curricula of Taiwan, Japan and the US, and it is made for young players: **Easy holds only first-step content** and the harder kinds live behind Harder and Super. Every kind is one tap on a number button, so a child never needs to read; each question is spoken. Bigger or smaller uses number choices only, with no picture; make ten and the sums within 20 use drawn ten-frames.

| Kind | Easy | Harder | Super | Clips |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bigger or smaller (`compare`) | the biggest of three numbers, 0–20 | the biggest of four numbers, 0–99 (often with digit-swapped neighbours, such as 34 and 43) | 0–99, biggest and smallest taking turns | `math2-bigger`, `math2-smaller` |
| What comes next (`sequence`) | count forward by 1s to 20 or 10s to 100 | by 2s to 20, 5s and 10s to 100; next or a missing middle number | count backward with mixed 1/2/5/10 steps; next or missing | `math2-sequence-next`, `math2-sequence-missing`, `math2-sequence-jumps`, `math2-sequence-backward` |
| Make ten (`make-ten`) | a ten-frame with 1–9 counters: "How many more make ten?" | — | — | `math2-ten-more` |
| Missing addend (`missing-addend`) | — | the ten-frame plus "3 + ? = 10" | the equation only, with the blank first or second ("? + 3 = 10") | `math2-ten-missing` |
| Tens and ones, count the blocks (`blocks`) | none | count rods (tens) and cubes (ones) to 99, from four choices | the same | `math2-blocks` |
| Tens and ones, ten more or ten less (`ten-more-less`) | none | none | the number shown as a numeral; find ten more or ten less, taking turns | `math2-tens-more`, `math2-tens-less` |
| Sums within 20 (`add-within-20`) | — | two ten-frames and "8 + 5 = ?" | the equation only | `math2-add-<a>-<b>` (36) |
| Take-aways within 20 (`take-within-20`) | — | a full frame plus the ones and "13 − 6 = ?", the counters taken away crossed out | the equation only | `math2-take-<from>-<n>` (36) |

The two comparison prompts are shared by every comparison question ("Which number is the biggest?" / 「哪一個數字最大？」 / 「いちばんおおきいかずは、どれかな？」, and the same with smallest). In Mandarin they use 大 and 小, the words for numbers (多 and 少 are kept for groups). The spoken prompt never gives the answer. Easy and Harder ask only for the biggest, so a first player meets one idea at a time; the direction changes on adjacent Super comparison questions.

**Tens and ones** (`blocks`, drawn by the `blocks` builder in `math2-visuals.js`) shows a rod for every ten, drawn as ten joined squares, and a cube for every one, in rows of five. A picture holds at most 9 rods and 9 cubes in one fixed drawing area, so 99 stays readable on a phone and every cube is the same size in every question. Easy has none of it, so a first player never meets it; Harder counts the blocks; Super adds "ten more / ten less", where the start number is a numeral and the spoken prompt says "this number". The wrong choices are the mistakes a child makes: the digits the other way round (34 for 43), one rod too many or too few, and one cube too many or too few. The spoken prompts never give the answer. In Mandarin they use 大 and 小, as for any number, and Japanese says じゅうおおきい / じゅうちいさい.

Adding a kind needs no change to the shared code:

- **`game.js`**: append one entry to `MATH2_KINDS` — its `kind`, the clip ids it can ask for (`audioIds`) and one generator per level it appears at. A generator returns a draft: `key`, `audioId`, `answer`, `choices` (numbers, the answer among them), `range` (`{ min, max }`, bounding every choice, so a kind can reach 20 or 99 without moving Math's limit of ten), the three prompts, and optionally `display` and `visual`. The topic rotates kinds like Math rotates plus and minus (the same kind is never asked three times in a row while another is on offer); the same question is never asked twice in a row.
- **`visual`** is plain data (`{ type: 'numerals', values: [...] }`) and nothing else: no markup and no functions. `app.js` hands it to `math2-visuals.js`, whose `BUILDERS` registry holds one function per `type` (`(visual, document) => Element`). It holds plain numerals, ten-frames, base-ten blocks and sequence rows; a picture such as a clock is one more builder there. A type with no builder is skipped.
- **Narration**: add the clip text to `audio/prompts.json`, generate it with `generate_gemini_audio.py --clip <id> --confirm`, check each clip by transcribing it back, and run `node monster-word-arena-tw/generate-precache-manifest.js` (never edit `precache-manifest.js` by hand).
- **Tests**: `game.test.js` checks that the bundled prompts are exactly the Math, word and Math 2 registry clips, and that the game reaches every one of them, both read from `MATH2_KINDS`; a new kind adds no edit to either. Its own behaviour goes in `math2.test.js`.

The six clips for bigger or smaller and the nine for tens and ones were transcribed back with whisper.cpp (large-v3-turbo); all three languages matched the manifest text (Mandarin came back in simplified script, the same as the other clips, and ten came back as "10" in English and Japanese).

### Make ten

The ten-frame (`{ type: 'ten-frame', counts: [3] }`) is drawn in SVG, two rows of five cells, with solid orange counters filling the top row first and then the bottom row, so five is a full top row. Empty cells are pale, so a child counts the counters and not the gaps. `counts` lists one entry per frame (0 to 10 counters each); the builder draws several frames in a row, as the [sums within 20](#within-20) do. The first addend (1–9) is the number of counters; the answer is what is missing from ten. Easy asks how many more; Harder and Super ask for a missing addend, with Super sometimes putting the blank first. The spoken prompts never contain the answer, and each of the two is one clip shared by every question ("How many more make ten?" / 「還要再加幾個，就湊成十？」 / 「あといくつで、じゅうになるかな？」, and "Which number fills the blank to make ten?" / 「空格裡要放哪一個數字，才能湊成十？」 / 「あいているところにいれると、じゅうになるかずは、どれかな？」). Easy offers the answer and its closest neighbours; Harder and Super half the time offer the number already shown (the answer to "how many are there?" rather than "how many more?"), the likely wrong tap. The six clips were transcribed back with whisper.cpp (large-v3-turbo). English and Japanese matched; the first Mandarin "missing" wording 填進空格 came back as 田径空格, so it was reworded to 空格裡要放…, regenerated once, and then matched (Mandarin comes back in simplified script, as for the other clips).

### Within 20

Sums and take-aways that cross ten, the make-ten strategy of grade 1: Easy never asks them, Harder shows two ten-frames with the equation, and Super shows the equation alone. Both numbers being added or taken away run from 2 to 9 and the take-away's answer is 2 to 9 as well, so every problem crosses ten (36 sums such as 8 + 5, from 2 + 9 to 9 + 9, and 36 take-aways such as 13 − 6, from 11 − 2 to 18 − 9). The picture is the same `ten-frame` builder as make ten, with three optional extras, all plain data:

- A sum draws its two frames with a "+" between them (`sign`). The counters that fill the fuller frame to ten are teal, and the empty cells they would land in are dashed teal (`move: { from, to, count }`), so for 8 + 5 the child sees two of the five counters ready to move into the frame of eight, without being told the answer. When the second number is the larger, its frame is the one that gets filled (2 + 9 moves the one counter from the frame of two).
- A take-away draws a full frame and the ones (`counts: [10, 3]` for 13 − 6) and fades and crosses out the counters being taken away (`taken: [3, 3]`): the ones first, then some of the full frame.
- A frame count, a move or a taken count that does not fit its frames is ignored or clamped, never drawn wrongly.

Each problem has its own spoken prompt, so the clip names both numbers but never the answer ("Eight plus five. How many altogether?" / 「八加五，總共是多少？」 / 「はちたすごは、ぜんぶでいくつかな？」; "Thirteen minus six. How many are left?" / 「十三減六，還剩下多少？」 / 「じゅうさんひくろくは、のこりはいくつかな？」), the same wording Math uses. That is 72 keys, `math2-add-<a>-<b>` and `math2-take-<from>-<n>`, or 216 clips in three languages; the on-screen prompt stays the short "How many altogether?" / "How many are left?" as it is in Math. Choices run from 0 to 20 and, on Harder and Super, half the time include the slip a child is likely to make: the sum without its ten (3 for 8 + 5) or ten minus the answer for a take-away (3 for 13 − 6).

All 216 clips were generated with `generate_gemini_audio.py` and transcribed back with whisper.cpp (large-v3-turbo) against the manifest text. English and Japanese matched every clip (the transcriber writes numbers as digits, "all together" for "altogether", and Japanese in its usual kanji spellings, so numbers were compared as numbers); Mandarin matched apart from three "十三減五" and "十五減…" clips where the number was misheard (十三 as 十三兼, 十五 as 食物), which were regenerated and then matched (one needed a third try).
Sequence cards show four text tiles in counting order, with one dashed `?` tile, and the child taps its number below. Easy always hides the last tile; Harder and Super may hide the last tile or either middle tile. Each row has one constant step (Super mixes backward steps between questions, not ambiguous changing steps inside a row), and skip-counting starts on a multiple of that step. The four shared prompts ask for next, missing, counting in equal jumps, or counting backward: the count-by prompt covers 2s, 5s and 10s without reading the sequence or its answer. This keeps narration to twelve clips, generated with the repository's usage-logged Gemini tool. All twelve were back-transcribed with whisper.cpp (large-v3-turbo). English and Mandarin matched (apart from Chinese script and punctuation). Both Whisper and Gemini heard the Japanese invitation かぞえよう as かぞえよ, even after a kanji-input retry; it was changed to かぞえてね, regenerated, and matched by both transcribers. Its audio input uses kanji while the screen stays hiragana. The other Japanese clips matched. The sequence row stays visible on Super because it is the question itself, not a countable-picture hint.

## Research notes

### Sourced findings

- The National Association for the Education of Young Children (NAEYC) describes developmentally appropriate practice as strengths-based, play-based joyful learning, with meaningful child choice and agency. Its guidance also discusses repeated practice and culturally/linguistically affirming environments. [DAP: Teaching to Enhance Each Child’s Development and Learning](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/dap/enhance-development/)
- NAEYC’s early-childhood article gives an example of using a joyful game to reinforce preschool numeracy. [The Power of Playful Learning in the Early Childhood Setting](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/pubs/yc/summer2022/power-playful-learning)
- Taiwan’s National Academy for Educational Research / Ministry of Education dictionaries use Traditional Chinese and list the selected Taiwan Mandarin terms: [small dictionary](https://dict.mini.moe.edu.tw/) (for example, 眼、鼻、耳、臉, and color entries) and the [family relationship table in the Concised Mandarin Chinese Dictionary](https://dict.concised.moe.edu.tw/appendix.jsp?ID=12&la=1&powerMode=0), which includes 爸爸、媽媽、哥哥、弟弟、姊姊、妹妹.

### Design judgment

Research suggests the value of play, choice, practice, and linguistic relevance; it does not prescribe this game’s specific rules. The short rounds, star-count finish, gentle feedback and second-chance tap after a miss, selectable champion and learning topic, and pretend power moves with a wobble-and-giggle reaction in place of hits are design choices intended to keep the play legible, encouraging, and gentle for a five-year-old. Vocabulary prompts use Taiwan Mandarin (not Taiwanese Hokkien) in Traditional Chinese characters and familiar Taiwan forms (including 姊姊, 橘色, 咖啡色, and 鳳梨). The easy/harder/super choice defaults to Easy so the first match always matches the original difficulty. Play-testing showed that the original picture-assisted word questions made the game too easy, closer to a three-year-old's level than a five-year-old's. The revised difficulty progression, miss handling, math pool, and sticker rules described above are design choices intended to encourage real thinking without taking back collected rewards, which can upset young children. A wrong tap loses a heart even when there are no stars to lose; running out of hearts ends the match, while abandoning a match after a miss also requires facing the lost-match card before starting fresh. Plus and minus take turns so neither can be missed. Four new topics (vegetables, flowers, vehicles, weather) give fresh, equally everyday vocabulary alongside animals, which young players already know well. The progressive reveal (champion, then topic and level with Start, then the arena) keeps the initial screen simple for a first-time child and gives one clear next step at a time, while still needing only three taps before the first question is heard.

## Stickers and costumes

A reason to replay that works like levelling up: winning a match (three right answers on Easy, five on Harder, ten on Super) earns the next of 12 stickers (star, rainbow, heart, balloon, sun, medal, cupcake, bubbles, flower, moon, dino egg, lollipop), shown on the finish screen and collected in the **Sticker book**, but only up to a **daily cap for each pairing of language and level**. After all 12, further paid wins add a ×2, ×3 count to each sticker in turn, up to a storage-safety limit of 9,999 recorded wins; later wins do not add stickers. The 2nd, 4th, 6th, and 8th stickers unlock a crown, party hat, flower crown, and propeller cap; each unlock is put on the champion who just won, and the sticker book lets the child dress Rex or Bobo in any unlocked costume or take it off. Rewards have no timers or purchase requirements, and a miss never removes a sticker or a costume (it takes a heart and, when available, a star from the match in play). In browsers without `showModal()`, the book degrades to a non-modal panel with no backdrop; the rest of the page remains interactive, and its Close button dismisses it.

**The daily cap.** Staying on one language at the easy level could otherwise earn stickers all day, so each win pays one sticker only while that pairing is under its cap for the device's local date:

| Level | Stickers per day, per language |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | 2 |
| Harder | 4 |
| Super | no cap |

"The language the player chose" is the main **language picker** at the top of the page (English, 繁體中文, or にほんご), the one control a child uses: it sets the on-screen text and, by default, the spoken voice, so it is what makes a question easy or hard to understand. The grown-up-only settings (a smaller second language and a different narration voice) do not count as a separate language for the cap. Switching to a harder level, or to another language, lets the player earn again straight away; the counts reset at local midnight on the device (`daily.date` in the saved value is the device's local date, and a saved tally from any other date is ignored). Every level pays one sticker per win, so a harder level pays at least as much as easy while leaving more room.

A **capped win** still celebrates with the same power move, cheer, and finish screen, then shows a friendly note in place of a sticker and speaks the same message in the voice language (`audio/reactions.json`, `reaction-cap-*`): "Great win! To earn your next sticker, try a harder level or another language." The note names only what would actually earn a sticker: a level the grown-up level lock has hidden is never suggested (so with only Easy unlocked it says to try another language), and when nothing is left today in any language and allowed level it says that is all the stickers for today and to come back tomorrow. A capped win adds no sticker and counts toward no costume, so it never moves the sticker book. If the break nudge is also due, the capped-win message is the one spoken, and the break prompt still shows.

A costume is a small overlay placed just above the champion picture in the arena and on the champion card, so it keeps working if the champion art changes. Rewards live in `rewards.js` (saved-state rules) and `rewards-app.js` (sticker book, finish-screen note, and overlays); `app.js` only calls `window.ArenaRewards?.recordWin(state.champion, { language, level, allowedLevels })` when a match is won (the result says whether a sticker was paid or the win was capped, and where the next one can come from) and `window.ArenaRewards?.setLanguage(textLanguage, secondLanguage)` on every language change, so the game plays normally without them and the sticker book follows the same chosen language and second language as the rest of the page. Every sticker and costume has an English, Traditional Chinese, and Japanese name as text; the pictures carry the current language's name as alt text inside elements hidden from screen readers, and each falls back to an emoji (⭐ 🌈 💖 🎈 ☀️ 🏅 🧁 🫧 🌼 🌙 🥚 🍭 👑 🎉 🌸 🧢) if its picture cannot load.

## Local speech assets

`audio/prompts.json` is the source for question prompts, including the [Math 2](#math-2) prompts, and `audio/reactions.json` is the source for the spoken reactions. All prompt, reaction, and word clips are generated at build time with [Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS](https://aistudio.google.com/learn/gemini-3-8-flash-tts-developer-guide): English uses the child-friendly Aoede voice, Taiwan Mandarin uses Kore with the `zh-TW` locale and explicit Taiwan-Mandarin direction, and Japanese uses the `ja-jp-tutor-1` Tokyo Japanese voice. Every Harder counting question shares one `math-count` clip ("How many eggs? Let's count!"), so the narration never says the answer; Super math draws only from the same sums and take-aways as Harder, so it needs no clip of its own. Every level and topic narrates the same question sentence (for example `colors-red` always says "Find red!"); the level changes only what the screen shows alongside the narration (a picture, a written word, or neither), not what is spoken. The Mandarin `family-sister` audio input uses 姐姐 (*jiějie*) to avoid Gemini reading 姊姊 with 姊's literary *zǐ* pronunciation; the visible prompt and answer label remain 姊姊. Three more clips use the same override mechanism after transcription found misreadings: the Mandarin `animals-cat` input uses 猫 because 貓 was read with a rising *máo* instead of the dictionary's level *māo* (the screen still shows 小貓), and the Japanese `face-mouth` and `fruit-pineapple` inputs use お口を見つけてね！ and パイナップルを見つけてね！ because the all-kana くち and パイナップル were misread. The reaction lines avoid words Gemini tends to misread, such as 對打 before 小, which tone sandhi turns into a 對答-sounding *duì dá*. Every reaction clip (including the two heart lines, transcribed with whisper.cpp large-v3-turbo) was checked by transcription, with pinyin for Mandarin and a kana reading for Japanese. Every new question clip was checked by transcribing it back with whisper.cpp (large-v3-turbo) against its manifest text; one Mandarin clip (`vegetables-cabbage`) was misheard on the first transcription pass and was regenerated once, then reverified. The 273 new math clips and 4 capped-win lines were each checked in all three languages. English and Taiwan Mandarin were transcribed with whisper.cpp (large-v3-turbo) against the manifest text (only numerals written as digits, "all together" for "altogether", and the minus sign written as "-" differed, none an actual misreading); the Mandarin clips and all Japanese clips were also transcribed by Gemini as pronounced (pinyin with tone marks for Mandarin, kana for Japanese), which confirmed 減 as *jiǎn*, 還 as *hái*, 更 as *gèng*, 張 as *zhāng*, the number words as read, and, for Japanese, the particle は sounded as わ. No clip needed regenerating or a pronunciation override. The math clips use the same Aoede, Kore, and `ja-jp-tutor-1` voices and the same unhurried pace as the existing prompts. `audio/prompts.json`'s Japanese text is chosen only for correct Gemini pronunciation and may use kanji or katakana (for example the `fruit-pineapple` override above). It is a separate, build-time-only concern from the on-screen Japanese in `game.js` and `i18n.js`, which is always hiragana for a pre-reading child to read aloud; the two can differ in spelling for the same word (くち on screen for `face-mouth`, お口 in the audio input) without disagreeing on how the word sounds.

To regenerate clips, install `ffmpeg`, provide `GEMINI_JOHN_API_KEY` through a private build environment, and confirm the paid API requests:

```sh
python3 monster-word-arena-tw/generate_gemini_audio.py --confirm
```

Without `--overwrite`, only missing clips are generated. `--overwrite` regenerates existing clips, `--clip <clip-id>` (repeatable) limits generation to selected prompt, reaction, or word clips, and `--language en|zh|ja` (repeatable) limits languages. For example, regenerate every English and Taiwan Mandarin prompt, reaction, and word clip with Gemini:

```sh
python3 monster-word-arena-tw/generate_gemini_audio.py --overwrite --language en --language zh --confirm
```

Check current [Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing) before authorizing generation. Only the fixed prompt, reaction, and word text is sent to Gemini during build-time generation. Never commit or expose the key. Speech is optional: language selection, replay, and mute are available in-game, while answer buttons remain usable if playback is unavailable.

## Sound effects and music

`sounds.js` synthesizes every effect and the music with the Web Audio API at play time: no audio files, stock or copyrighted samples, or network requests, and about 10 KB of code. The effects are short and non-speech: a soft tap when the child answers or presses a game button, rising twinkles for a right answer, an airy whoosh with the power move, a tiny "hee-hee" wobble for the sparring buddy, a bouncy pillow boing for a miss, a small fanfare at the win, and a gentle two-note chime whenever Rex or Bobo points at the next thing to tap. They start from the child's own taps, so they need no voice choice first.

Optional background music (a slow pentatonic lullaby loop) is off by default and has its own **🎵 Music · 音樂** toggle. The existing mute button silences everything: narration, effects, and music. Music stops while the page is hidden and resumes when it returns.

Effects and music play on their own `AudioContext`, never on the shared speech `<audio>` element, so they cannot cut off a question or spoken reaction. They also duck further while speech plays. Rendered offline in Chrome, each effect's loudest 50 ms sits about 14–24 dB below the narration clips, and the music sits about 25 dB below them. To keep sounds from piling up during rapid tapping, repeats of the same effect inside a short gap are dropped and a retriggered effect replaces the one still ringing, so each effect rings at most once at a time. Without Web Audio, the game stays silent and fully playable.
